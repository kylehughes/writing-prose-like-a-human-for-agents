# Writing prose like a human: reference

Use this reference when a particular phrase or structure needs judgment. It is a set of editing questions, not a blacklist. The five rules and preservation constraints in [SKILL.md](SKILL.md) govern every edit.

## Vocabulary in context

A familiar phrase can be precise, conventional in a field, or part of the author's voice. Replace it only when the replacement improves the passage without changing its meaning. Repeated generic wording may deserve attention; its presence does not establish who wrote the text.

| Language to inspect | Ask | Possible edit when meaning permits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| pivotal, crucial, vital, key, watershed | Does the passage explain the importance? | State the supplied effect, use "important," or remove empty emphasis. |
| testament, lasting legacy, indelible mark | What evidence or continuing effect is actually given? | Name that evidence or effect; do not invent one. |
| broader trends, evolving landscape, tapestry | Is there a specific relationship here? | Name the supplied trend or relationship, or remove a generic gloss. |
| delve, garner, leverage, facilitate | Is the formal word doing useful work? | Consider "examine," "receive," "use," or "help" only if equivalent. |
| serves as, stands as, represents | Is this identity, function, symbolism, or a temporary role? | Use "is" for identity; preserve other meanings. |
| showcase, boast, renowned | Does the genre call for promotion or attributed praise? | Use a neutral description for factual reporting; retain intentional voice. |
| robust, seamless, comprehensive | Is the property defined or supported? | Keep a technical meaning; otherwise describe the supported property. |
| rich, vibrant, stunning, intricate | Does the modifier communicate a useful observation or judgment? | Keep that observation, or remove interchangeable decoration. |
| additionally, furthermore, moreover, however | Does the transition express a real logical connection? | Keep it if useful; vary repeated openings only when that helps flow. |
| it is worth noting, at its core | Does the framing help the reader? | Often begin with the point itself. |
| not only X but also Y; not X but Y | Is addition or contrast the intended relationship? | Keep meaningful contrast; simplify a routine addition to "X and Y." |
| experts say, reports suggest | Is the attribution supported and as specific as the material allows? | Retain uncertainty; name an available source without inventing one. |
| such as, including, from X to Y | Is the list partial, exhaustive, or illustrating breadth? | Preserve that distinction; do not silently turn examples into a complete list. |
| based on available information, may, appears | Does the qualification limit the claim? | Remove redundant framing only; retain the actual evidentiary limit. |

Literal and specialized meanings matter: a tapestry can be a textile, a key can unlock a door, leverage can describe borrowing, and a robust estimator is a technical term. Do not run global find-and-replace over these words.

## Fact-preserving examples

These are invented teaching passages, not factual claims about real institutions. Each revision uses only information in its input. Removed praise is unsupported editorial framing; no revision supplies a replacement fact from outside the passage.

### 1. Specificity and significance

**Before:**
> The building was renovated in 1987, a pivotal moment that stands as a testament to the community's enduring legacy.

**After:**
> The building was renovated in 1987.

The input gives no cost, funding source, reason, or result. Adding a roof collapse or council grant would fabricate evidence. If the surrounding material explains why the renovation mattered, preserve that explanation instead of cutting it by default.

### 2. Plain verbs without losing the object

**Before:**
> The tool serves as a validation mechanism for user inputs before submission.

**After:**
> The tool validates user inputs before submission.

The revision keeps what is validated and when. By contrast, "The hall serves as a shelter during floods" describes a conditional use; "The hall is a shelter" would broaden the claim.

### 3. Empty commentary versus a useful consequence

**Before:**
> The company opened a second factory in 2019, underscoring its enduring commitment to excellence.

**After:**
> The company opened a second factory in 2019.

The trailing praise supplies no concrete consequence. Compare a clause that does:

**Before:**
> The valve closes when pressure exceeds the limit, preventing further flow into the chamber.

**After:**
> The valve closes when pressure exceeds the limit, preventing further flow into the chamber.

No edit is needed. Removing the participial clause would discard the stated effect.

### 4. Rhythm without replacing the content

**Before:**
> Not only does the festival celebrate local traditions, but it also presents new artists. Additionally, it attracts visitors from across the region. Furthermore, it supports local vendors.

**After:**
> The festival celebrates local traditions and presents new artists. It attracts visitors from across the region and supports local vendors.

All four claims remain. The edit adds no schedule, attendance estimate, or vendor count. Keep the original parallelism if it better suits the intended emphasis.

### 5. Modifiers with and without work to do

**Before:**
> The bustling market has 30 food stalls and two covered seating areas, offering a truly unparalleled culinary experience.

**After:**
> The busy market has 30 food stalls and two covered seating areas.

"Busy" preserves the supplied observation in plainer language. The unsupported superlative is removed. This does not authorize deleting concrete descriptions merely because they are adjectives.

### Uncertainty and attribution

**Before:**
> Based on the available inspection notes, the leak may have started before June; the exact date has not been established.

**After:**
> The inspection notes suggest the leak may have started before June; the exact date has not been established.

The evidence remains limited. "The leak started before June" would falsely increase certainty. "The date is unknown" can also overstate the gap: the date may be known elsewhere but absent from these notes.

### Precision before vividness

**Before:**
> The battle resulted in approximately 3,000 casualties.

**After:**
> The battle caused about 3,000 casualties.

Do not change "casualties" to "deaths," specify soldiers rather than civilians, or add a duration. Those would change the claim.

### Stable terminology

**Before:**
> The bridge opened in 1910. The structure connects the north and south banks. The span carries about 20,000 vehicles per day.

**After:**
> The bridge opened in 1910. It connects the north and south banks and carries about 20,000 vehicles per day.

Repeat a noun when that prevents ambiguity. Do not force repetition where distinct terms describe different things.

## Structure and formatting

Choose the form that serves the document and its explicit requirements.

- **Summaries and conclusions:** Cut redundant recaps when they slow a short passage. Keep requested summaries and conclusions that synthesize an argument, record a decision, or help navigation.
- **Challenges and outlook:** Replace a generic optimism arc with the actual problems and supported prospects. Preserve real commitments, uncertainties, and plans; do not invent a positive ending or erase a supported one.
- **Lists and tables:** Use lists for steps or parallel items and tables for comparisons or mappings. Do not convert either just because it is short, has three entries, or uses bold labels. Preserve requested checklists and templates.
- **Headings and typography:** Follow the author's or project's convention for capitalization, quotation marks, emphasis, and punctuation. There is no useful per-page quota for em dashes or other marks. Change punctuation to improve a sentence, not to meet a supposed authorship signal.
- **Range and breadth:** "From biology to politics" can illustrate breadth without describing a numerical scale. Revise only if it creates a misleading scope claim or obscures the actual subjects.
- **Conversational language:** Remove assistant preambles and offers from a standalone article when they do not belong there. Keep greetings, warmth, first person, and questions when writing a message that calls for them.
- **Caveats and care:** Separate generic framing from necessary limitations, safety information, consent conditions, and considerate language. Preserve what changes how the reader should understand or act on the text.

## Final comparison

Check the revision against its input, not against a target word count or detector score:

- Are dates, quantities, names, technical distinctions, citations, and list membership preserved?
- Does it retain the same certainty, conditions, attribution, and intended judgment?
- Did a new concrete detail, causal explanation, or quotation enter without evidence?
- Is the voice still appropriate to this author, audience, and genre?
- Are explicit formatting, code, frontmatter, structured data, and links intact?
- Does each cut improve clarity without removing something the reader needs?
